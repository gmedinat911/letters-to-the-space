LETTER I OF VI

Maybe Tomorrow

Dear you,

I caught myself doing something the other day that made me sadder than it probably should have.

I opened our messages because I wanted to tell you something. I don't even remember what it was anymore. Probably something stupid. Something that would have made absolutely no sense to anyone else.

But before I typed anything, I scrolled up.

I wanted to see who had texted last.

I actually did that.

Like there was some kind of rule now. Like I needed to make sure I wasn't reaching out too much, or that enough time had passed since the last time we talked.

And I hated that.

Because when did talking to you become something I had to think about?

There was a time when I could send you absolutely anything. Something funny, something ridiculous, something that happened during my day. I didn't need a reason. I didn't have to wonder whether you wanted to hear from me.

I just knew I could.

And maybe that's what I've been missing lately.

That certainty.

Not the certainty that you'd always answer, or that we'd talk every day. I never needed that.

Just knowing I had a little place in your life where I didn't have to think so much about whether I belonged.

I don't know when that started feeling different.

And the frustrating part is that nothing particularly bad has happened.

You're still kind to me.

We still talk.

Sometimes we still laugh like nothing has changed.

And for a moment, I forget that I've been feeling this way.

Until another day comes along, and I catch myself wondering again.

I think what scares me is that I don't know whether you're feeling any of this.

Maybe to you, everything is completely normal. Maybe life is just busy, and I'm sitting here trying to find meaning in something that doesn't have any.

God, I hope that's true.

But then there's another part of me that hopes you've noticed too.

Not because I want you to feel bad.

I think I just want to know that whatever has been changing between us isn't something I've been experiencing alone.

I don't particularly like admitting that.

It feels selfish somehow.

And maybe I'm making this bigger than it needs to be.

I don't know.

I just know that I miss how easy it used to be.

I miss not having to think before saying hello.

I miss being able to share something completely unimportant without wondering if I was becoming one more thing you had to answer.

And I miss the version of me who didn't question any of that.

Anyway.

I never sent the message.

I still could, I guess.

Maybe tomorrow.

And I think that's the part that hurts a little.

That with you, of all people, there's a maybe where there never used to be one.

LETTER II OF VI

The Things I Almost Tell You

Dear you,

I saw something today and thought of you before I even had time to think.

It was stupid. The kind of thing I would have sent you with no explanation because you would have known exactly why it made me laugh.

For a second, I reached for my phone.

Then I put it down.

I used to think missing someone was about the big things. Birthdays. Important news. The moments when you look around and wish a certain person were standing beside you.

But lately, it's been smaller than that.

It's hearing a joke and knowing the face you'd make. It's wanting to complain about a perfectly ordinary Tuesday. It's seeing something you would love and realizing I don't know if we're still the sort of people who send each other things like that.

The funny part is, you probably would have answered. You might even have laughed.

I don't actually know that anything bad would happen.

I just miss not having to wonder.

I don't want every little message to become a question about where I stand. That would take all the fun out of sharing it.

Still, my mind hasn't quite learned that our days don't overlap the way they used to.

I keep collecting these tiny things without meaning to.

A story. A song. Something ridiculous someone said.

None of them is important enough to make a big deal out of. But together, they remind me of how much of a friendship lives in the things we never think to remember.

I hope you have people to tell your small stories to.

And I hope, eventually, I'll stop feeling so strange about the ones I don't tell you.

For now, I still wish I could have sent you that stupid thing.

You would have laughed.

LETTER III OF VI

When Talking Became Different

Dear you,

We still talk sometimes.

That should make this easier to explain. It doesn't.

There's nothing wrong with those conversations. We ask about each other's days. We laugh at something. We say we'll catch up. If someone overheard us, I doubt they would notice anything different.

But I notice how careful I've become.

I decide which stories are worth sending. I wonder whether I've already been the one to reach out lately. I reread perfectly normal replies as though there might be an answer hiding in them.

Then I catch myself doing it and feel a little ridiculous.

It's just a conversation.

Except talking to you used to be somewhere I could arrive exactly as I was.

I didn't have to think about the rhythm. We could let a conversation wander and trust that it would find its way.

Now even the nice ones can leave me feeling strangely far away.

I don't think you've done something wrong. People get busy. Their lives change. Sometimes two people find themselves moving at different speeds without either one choosing it.

And maybe part of what hurts is that I'm still listening for a version of us that isn't here in the same way anymore.

I don't want to turn a good conversation into a disappointment just because it isn't the conversation I remember.

I want to appreciate what we still have, whatever shape that takes.

I just haven't quite figured out how to do that without missing what felt so easy before.

Maybe that's the difference.

We still know how to talk.

I'm just learning how to stop listening for the distance.

LETTER IV OF VI

I Wonder If You Notice

Dear you,

Sometimes I wonder whether you notice how long it's been since we really talked.

Not in some dramatic way. I don't expect the world to stop because I'm not there.

I just wonder whether something funny happens and, for a second, you think of telling me. Whether you've ever opened our messages and decided to write later. Whether anything ordinary still reminds you of me.

I know those are questions I might never get answers to.

And I wouldn't know how to ask them without making you feel as though you owe me something.

I don't want you to prove that our friendship mattered by being sad about it. It mattered to me already.

But there's a fear I don't particularly like admitting.

What if this distance is only happening on my side?

What if I'm slowly learning how to live with a change you've barely noticed?

I don't like that thought. Not because I want you to hurt too, but because I want to believe we both felt something worth keeping.

Then I remind myself that people don't have to miss each other in the same way for the care to have been real.

Maybe you think we're fine. Maybe you have so much going on that the weeks have simply slipped by. Maybe your idea of closeness was always a little different from mine.

I can understand all of those possibilities.

Understanding them doesn't make the uncertainty disappear.

So I guess this is one of the things I have to learn to carry without asking you to solve it.

I hope you think of me sometimes.

Not because I need you to come back.

Just because it would be nice to know that, somewhere in your ordinary days, a small part of our friendship is still there.

LETTER V OF VI

Learning Not to Reach

Dear you,

I didn't write to you today.

Not because I was upset. And not because I wanted you to notice that I hadn't.

I almost did.

But when I sat with it for a minute, I realized I wasn't really reaching for a conversation. I was reaching for a feeling.

I wanted to feel that we were okay. That I still had a place. That I hadn't somehow imagined how close we once were.

That's a lot to ask a simple hello to give me.

And I don't want every message to turn into a test you don't even know you're taking.

So I went on with my day.

I made coffee. I got caught up in other things. I laughed at something completely unrelated. At some point, I realized I hadn't checked my phone in a while.

It wasn't a breakthrough. I still missed you.

But there was something comforting about letting the day belong to me.

I'm learning that giving someone space isn't the same as closing the door. And stepping back doesn't mean taking back all the good feelings I have for them.

If you write, I'll be happy to hear from you.

If you don't, I want to be able to live with that without turning the silence into a verdict about myself.

I can keep caring about you.

I can also stop trying to hold both ends of a conversation.

I think I need to let both of those things be true.

For your sake, and for mine.

LETTER VI OF VI

A Different Kind of Missing

Dear you,

I thought missing you would either get louder or eventually disappear.

It did neither.

It became quieter.

There are still moments when something reminds me of you. I still feel that old instinct to tell you. But now I can have the thought, smile a little, and keep going with my day.

I used to worry that this meant I was forgetting.

I don't think it does.

I remember the laughter. I remember how easy it was to talk about nothing. I remember the ordinary moments I didn't know I'd end up missing.

I just don't need to replay them as often anymore.

I'm beginning to understand that being grateful for something doesn't mean asking for it to happen again.

And I don't know what comes next for us.

Maybe we'll find our way into a different kind of closeness. Maybe we'll surprise each other someday and pick up a conversation. Maybe this distance will simply become part of our lives.

I'm trying not to decide the ending for both of us.

What I hope is that if we meet again, there will be enough kindness between us to meet as we are, rather than pretending nothing has changed.

And if we don't, I hope neither of us remembers this as a failure.

Some things can be good and still change.

I still miss you.

Just differently now.

And there's something peaceful about being able to say that without asking you to do anything about it.
