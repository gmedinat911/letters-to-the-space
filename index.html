Letter · I
August 2, 2026

The Future I Had Already Met

Some people become part of your ordinary life before you realize how much space they occupy.

There are some people you get used to having around before you ever realize it happened.

You become familiar with the small things—the runs, the laughs, the conversations that wander nowhere, the version of someone who is focused one minute and completely ridiculous the next. None of it seems particularly important while it is happening. Then one day you imagine those things not being there, and suddenly they feel enormous.

I think that is what I finally understood.

I had quietly made room for you in parts of my life I hadn't even noticed yet.

And maybe I made too much room.

There is nothing wrong with you for that. You are still the same wonderfully genuine, kind, funny, beautifully imperfect person I got to know. This isn't about asking you to be anything different.

It's just me realizing that sometimes caring about someone also means knowing when to take a small step back.

That part hurts more than I expected.

Not because I want my absence to prove anything, and not because I expect the story to suddenly change. I think it hurts because somewhere along the way I had started assuming there would simply be more.

More runs. More laughing. More random conversations. More learning the tiny things nobody thinks to ask about—favorite colors, coffee orders, the things that scare you, the things that make you unexpectedly emotional.

More ordinary days.

I think those were the ones I was looking forward to most.

Maybe life circles people back toward each other. Maybe it doesn't. Either way, I am grateful I got to know this version of you.

Some people leave a bigger mark than they probably ever realize.

Letter · II
August 5, 2026

The Space Between Our Words

Sometimes the meaning isn’t in what was said, but in everything one person hoped the other would hear.

I went quiet for a little while.

Part of me wondered whether you would notice. Not in some dramatic way. I think I just wanted to know whether the space I normally occupied would feel any different when I wasn't standing in it.

Your last message was perfectly kind.

You were happy. You felt more confident. The day had given you some reassurance about what was ahead.

There was absolutely nothing wrong with what you said.

And somehow it still hurt.

Not because of the words themselves, but because of everything that wasn't inside them.

I think that was when I realized how much can exist in the space between what one person says and what another person hears.

I had watched you do something difficult and wanted you to know that I saw it—not just the result, but the work underneath it. The composure. The confidence you had earned. I wanted you to know I was proud of you.

I even slipped in the important things: tacos and ice cream.

You said thank you.

Kindly. Normally. Exactly as anyone could.

Maybe I had simply put more between my lines than anyone else could reasonably be expected to find there.

That's the strange thing about words. Two people can read the same sentence and be holding completely different letters.

So I went quiet.

Not to punish you. Not to make you come looking. Mostly because I needed to understand why something so small had managed to make me feel so empty.

I think I know now.

Sometimes you discover how much a person means to you by noticing how much meaning you have been hiding inside ordinary things.

A run is just a run.

Coffee is just coffee.

A race is just a race.

Unless, somehow, it isn't.

Letter · III
August 6, 2026

If a Leaf Falls Quietly

A question about being remembered, being noticed, and the weight of feeling invisible.

Will I be remembered if I disappear?

I always ask myself that question.

Will people notice? Or will I just be someone who wasn’t worth remembering?

I have spent so much of my life trying to make other people’s lives a little lighter. I smile. I listen. I care. I give pieces of myself freely, hoping that maybe one day someone will choose me with the same intention.

Maybe that expectation isn’t fair. Maybe people love differently.

But some days I can’t help feeling invisible.

Like I’m just a leaf passing through people’s lives—noticed for a moment, then forgotten the second the wind changes.

And that’s the part that hurts the most.

What if I were gone from their lives completely? What if one day I just vanished from the places where I’ve always been?

Would anyone notice the empty space? Would anyone miss me? Would anyone wish they had cared a little louder?

I don’t know.

Right now, I just can’t believe they would.

Maybe I’m not invisible. Maybe I’m just exhausted from always being the one who reaches first.

Letter · IV

Invisible :)

Some days the smile is the smallest part of the sentence.

My life feels emptier than others.

I feel trapped. Stuck. Like I’ve got nowhere to go.

There are moments when it hurts less—when it doesn’t really feel like anything at all. But no matter what I do, there’s this unhappiness, this emptiness, this feeling that something inside me is broken.

My whole heart feels empty.

I gave so much of it to you. To everyone. And somehow, I always ended up feeling taken for granted.

I don’t know why my heart sank so suddenly.

Maybe I’ve been resisting all of this for too long. Maybe everything I’ve been pushing down finally needed somewhere to go. Maybe this needed to happen.

I have no idea.

It just sucks feeling like I always have to make sense of everything. Everywhere. Every time.

Maybe this is just my life these days.

Broken.

Like I’m here—I’m going through the motions, I’m existing—but somehow it feels like everything that made being here meaningful is gone.

I hope one day I feel happy again.

Because right now, I just feel invisible to everyone else.

Letter · V
August 7, 2026

Three Dots

Some endings do not feel like endings. Sometimes there are only three dots.

Sometimes I don’t know how to start these letters.

But in your case, I think I’m just afraid of what comes next.

I feel like sometimes actions only come when it’s already too late. And no matter what happens after that, some things become harder to repair.

I felt alone.

For so long, I felt like no one—including you—really understood who I was. And I think that hurt the most.

Feeling like I was somehow less than everyone else. Like my love, or at least the way I showed it, meant less. Like it could go unnoticed until the moment when noticing it might not be enough anymore.

But one thing I can say with certainty is that I loved every single thing I did with you.

I loved the moments. The memories. The life we shared together.

I cared. And I cared a lot.

I was there when you needed me. I listened when I didn’t have to. I loved even when I felt like I didn’t have much love left inside me to give.

And I did all of it with an immense amount of love for you.

You said you felt it.

You said I was enough.

So why did I always feel like I wasn’t?

I’m too deep in the dark right now to know the answer. I’m exhausted from fighting it. Sometimes it feels like my soul is slowly fading.

Maybe because I gave it all.

Maybe because I didn’t say it all.

Maybe because somewhere along the way, I stopped standing up for myself.

But there is one thing I don’t question:

It wasn’t because I didn’t love you enough.

That, I am sure of.

Right now, I’m just feeling.

Hopeless.

Lonely.

Empty.

And maybe, for now, that’s okay.

I blame myself for some of it. I let too much get this far without saying what was happening inside me.

Maybe one day I’ll find my way out of here.

Maybe I’ll find myself again.

Until then...

Three dots.

Letter · VI
August 9, 2026

When I’m Ready

Sometimes the kindest sentences are the hardest ones to know what to do with.

I think some sentences hurt because they are kind.

There is nothing cruel about I’ll be here when you’re ready.

Maybe that’s why I haven’t known what to do with it.

Because I understand.

I understand that nobody should have to carry the weight of another person.

I understand that caring about someone doesn’t make you responsible for saving them.

I understand that sometimes space is the healthiest thing two people can give each other.

I understand all of it.

And somehow, it still hurts.

Because I don’t know what ready means.

Does it mean when I’m okay again?

When I’ve untangled everything?

When I can talk about my life without accidentally handing you pieces of it to carry?

When I don’t need reassurance that you’re still there?

Maybe.

But there’s a small part of me that heard something completely different.

Come back when you’re easier to hold.

And I know that’s probably unfair.

Those weren’t your words.

They were mine.

Or maybe they belonged to a much younger version of me who learned somewhere along the way that people are easier to keep when you don’t need too much from them.

So now I’m doing the thing you asked me to do.

I’m stepping back.

Not because I’m angry.

Not because I stopped caring.

God, I wish caring were that easy to turn off.

I’m stepping back because I care enough to understand that someone’s boundary isn’t an invitation to convince them otherwise.

But there’s something neither of us can know yet.

You said you’d be there when I’m ready.

And maybe you will.

I just don’t know who I’ll be when that happens.

Maybe I’ll come back and we’ll laugh about stupid things again.

Maybe everything will feel familiar.

Maybe I’ll finally understand what you meant.

Or maybe, somewhere between here and ready, I’ll learn how to stop leaving so much of myself in other people’s hands.

And when I finally look around, the space you once occupied will have changed.

Not because I wanted to erase you.

Not because what we had wasn’t real.

But because while I was learning how to stand on my own, I had to learn how to live without reaching for you.

That’s the strange thing about asking someone to come back when they’re ready.

Sometimes becoming ready changes where home is.

So I won’t ask you to wait.

And I won’t promise I’ll come back exactly the same.

I’ll just go do the work.

And if someday we find each other again,

I hope we recognize each other.

Letter · VII
August 10, 2026

Always Here

Sometimes reassurance can still feel strangely far away.

Always here for you.

I keep thinking about those words.

They should have made me feel better.

I think a part of me really wanted them to.

But somehow, they hurt.

Maybe because they came after being told to come back when I was ready.

And suddenly always here felt less like I’m staying, and more like I’ll be somewhere if you decide to come back.

Maybe that isn’t what you meant.

I hope it isn’t.

But I think what hurt was realizing how differently I understand the word here.

When I say I’m here, I mean you don’t have to disappear until life feels lighter.

I mean I’ll still send you something stupid.

I’ll still wonder how your day went.

I’ll still want you around even when you don’t have much of yourself to give.

I don’t need to fix you.

I don’t need to carry you.

I just don’t want you to feel like you have to become okay before you can sit beside me again.

Maybe always here means something different to you.

Maybe it simply means the door will still be open.

And maybe that should be enough.

But if I’m being truthful,

I didn’t need an open door.

I think I just needed to know you were still on the other side of it.

So I’ll believe the words the best way I know how.

I won’t ask you to prove them.

I won’t chase their meaning.

I’ll just let time show me where here actually is.

I just wish those beautiful words didn’t feel so far away.
