Someone Who Remembers

It’s strange trying to put this year into words because I don’t think I’ve ever had a year where I felt so much at once.

Some of the things I had dreamed about for years finally became real. I built things I once thought were impossibly far away. I came home to places and people I had missed for far too long. I traveled. I ran. I loved. I celebrated. I questioned things I thought I was certain about. I had moments where I felt completely full of life, and others where I felt like I was standing still while everything around me kept moving.

And somewhere in all of that, I think I met myself again.

Not a new version of me. Maybe just the version that had been there the whole time, underneath everything I thought I had to be.

This year I walked away from things that had become part of how I recognized myself. Places, routines, people, plans, even versions of my future that had once felt permanent. Some of those choices felt almost like tearing pieces off and looking underneath to see what was actually mine.

And maybe, somewhere underneath that, there was also a quieter question: if all of these things changed, who would still recognize me? Who would stay?

But eventually the question became less about who would stay and more about who was left underneath all of it.

Me.

I started paying attention again to the things that make me feel alive. The part of me that wants to run until my head gets quiet. The one that wants to get on a plane and see somewhere I’ve never seen before. But also the part of me that remembers. The one that notices the little things people say and somehow remembers them months or years later. The one that holds onto small moments that probably looked ordinary from the outside but meant something to me.

I think I had forgotten how much of myself lives in those details.

Because remembering has always been one of the ways I love.

And I don’t say this kind of thing about myself very often, but maybe 32 is a good place to start.

At my core, I think I’m soft.

I care deeply. Probably more deeply than I let most people see. I’m sentimental. I’m curious. I’m loyal. I get excited about small things. I ask questions because I genuinely want to know the answers.

When I care about someone, I pay attention. I remember their stories. I remember the random thing they told me months ago. I remember places, dates, songs, conversations, and little details because somewhere in my brain they became attached to that person.

I want the people I love to feel seen.

I will show up. I will give. I will worry. I will check in. I will probably remember something you forgot you ever told me.

And beneath whatever confidence I might show to the world, there is also someone who wants those same things back.

Someone who wants to feel remembered.

Someone who wants to know that his presence leaves something behind when he isn’t in the room. Someone who wants freedom fiercely, but also wants closeness. Someone independent enough to walk alone and soft enough to still hope someone chooses to walk beside him.

That person has always been there.

I think I just stopped listening to him for a while.

Maybe because some of those qualities are easier to carry when everything is going well. This year they also made things hurt.

Caring deeply means feeling absence deeply. Remembering means sometimes carrying things you wish your mind would put down. Hope can make silence feel louder than it probably is.

For a while I think I treated some of those things like they were parts of myself I needed to outgrow.

I don’t think that anymore.

I don’t want to become less caring just because caring can hurt. I don’t want to remember less because sometimes I wish other people remembered more. I don’t want to become less soft just because being soft occasionally makes the world heavier.

I’d rather learn where those parts of me belong.

And somehow, in a year when I didn’t always recognize myself, I found people who gave me a hand when I felt like I was falling. People who lifted me up without asking me to become someone easier to carry first. People who saw versions of me that were uncertain, insecure and very much unfinished, and stayed anyway.

That changed something in me too.

I think I used to imagine there would eventually be some more complete version of myself. One who had figured everything out. Who knew exactly what he wanted, made all the right decisions, and carried considerably less doubt.

I’m starting to think he doesn’t exist.

There is just me, as I am, becoming as I go.

And strangely, there’s a lot of freedom in that.

This year taught me that I can love something and still question it. That I can be grateful and still want more. That missing something doesn’t always mean I’m supposed to go looking for it. And that not everything meaningful is meant to follow us forever.

Some things stay in our lives. Others stay only in our memories.

Both can matter.

Maybe that’s what this year has been about.

Learning what to carry with me and what to leave where it belongs. Learning that letting go of a version of myself doesn’t mean that version was a mistake.

He got me here.

I can be grateful for him without needing to remain him.

So I got to turn 32 in Italy, surrounded by places that have survived thousands of years while still carrying the marks of everything they’ve been through.

There was something quietly beautiful about that.

If 31 left me with any conclusion, maybe it’s that I’ll never feel 100% ready. There probably won’t be some future version of me who has everything figured out, carries no doubt, and finally gives me permission to move.

And I think I’m okay with that.

Maybe being ready was never the point. Maybe I’m allowed to be uncertain, unfinished, and still keep going.

I’m just grateful I was here for all of it.

For the people who gave me their hand. For the ones who stayed. For the people I loved. For the dreams that became real. For the things that changed me. For the little moments I’ll probably remember forever. For every version of myself that got me here. For the parts of myself I found again.

And for everything I still haven’t met yet.

32 feels less like starting over and more like continuing—with a little more of myself this time.
