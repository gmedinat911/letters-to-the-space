For All the People I Couldn't Keep

Some constellations are only meant to hold for a little while.

I've been thinking about how strange it is to miss someone who is still somewhere in this world.

Someone who is still living their life, laughing, making memories, waking up every morning. Someone who hasn't really gone anywhere, except perhaps away from the place they once held in yours.

And I don't really know what to call that.

Maybe it's goodbye. Maybe it's acceptance. Or maybe it's simply learning that not everyone we love and care about is meant to walk beside us through every season of life.

Lately, I've been thinking about the people who made my life a little brighter just by being in it.

The conversations, the laughter, the stupid little moments that probably meant nothing to anyone watching, but somehow meant the world to me.

The ordinary days that became beautiful memories without us even realizing it.

And I am grateful.

God, I am so grateful.

But I would be lying if I said that gratitude makes the sadness disappear.

Sometimes I wish people could see themselves through my eyes. Just for a moment. To understand how precious they were to me. How much their presence meant. How genuinely happy I was to have them in my life.

Not because I needed them to make me happy.

But because I saw something beautiful in them, and I wanted to be there for it.

I've always cared deeply. Maybe sometimes more than I know how to express.

When someone becomes important to me, I want them to know it. I want to celebrate their victories, listen to their stories, share the stupid things that happen throughout my day, and simply be part of their lives.

Not every day. Not every moment.

Just somewhere.

I never wanted anyone to feel obligated to have me in their life. I just wanted to feel that they wanted me there.

And I think that's what hurts sometimes.

When a friendship that once felt so natural becomes something you start to question. When saying hello becomes something you overthink. When you wonder whether reaching out to someone you love will make them smile or make them feel bothered.

I never saw you as a burden, so it hurt to start wondering whether I had become one to you.

Maybe that wasn't true. Maybe it was my own fears making something out of the silence.

I don't know.

But I know how it felt.

There's something quietly heartbreaking about having a thought, a story, or something silly happen during your day, and wanting to tell someone who used to be part of those moments.

And then realizing you don't quite know if you should anymore.

Because friendship, at least to me, was never about how often we spoke or how much time we spent together.

It was about knowing we had a place in each other's lives.

A place we didn't constantly have to prove we deserved.

And I guess that's what I miss sometimes. Not necessarily the conversations or the time together, but how easy it once felt to be part of each other's worlds.

I wish I knew when that changed.

Or perhaps it didn't change as much as I think it did. Maybe we simply understood our friendship differently.

I'm realizing that people have their own ways of caring. Their own limits, their own lives, and their own understanding of what it means to be close to someone.

And sometimes, even when the care is real, it isn't enough to make two people feel the same way about a friendship.

Maybe neither of us has to be wrong for that to hurt.

I'm also learning that I don't have to become less affectionate, less thoughtful, or less willing to show up because something didn't turn out the way I hoped.

I can respect someone's need for distance and still feel sad about it.

I can miss someone without asking them to come back.

And I can be thankful for what we had without pretending I didn't wish we had more.

So, to the people who have been part of my life, who made ordinary days extraordinary without even realizing it:

Thank you.

For the conversations that made the hours disappear. For the laughter, the memories, and the little pieces of yourselves that you shared with me.

For letting me know you, even if only for a season.

I wish I could say I remember everything without a little sadness.

But some memories still hurt.

Some friendships ended before I was ready.

And some people left me wondering whether I meant as much to them as they meant to me.

Maybe I'll never know.

But I don't want those questions to take away from what was beautiful.

Because it was beautiful.

And it was real to me.

Even if things didn't end the way I wished they would.

I hope life is gentle with you.

I hope you find people who make you feel understood, appreciated, and wanted. People you can be yourself around without having to wonder whether you belong.

And if our paths never cross again, I hope you know that our time together mattered.

At least, it mattered to me.

And maybe that's enough.

I don't want to turn a beautiful memory into a painful one just because I wish there had been more of it.

I don't want the silence at the end to become the only thing I remember.

I'd rather remember the good.

The laughter.

The little moments.

The simple happiness of having known you.

And perhaps the hardest part of all is learning that I can let people go without believing their leaving means I wasn't worth staying for.

Because I was.

Maybe this isn't goodbye.

Maybe it's simply making peace with the fact that some people will always have a place in our stories, even if they no longer have a place in our everyday lives.

And maybe that's okay.

Maybe the beautiful thing about having known someone isn't how long they stayed, but that, for a little while, our lives found each other.

And we got to share a small piece of this strange, wonderful experience of being alive.

So wherever you are, whatever life looks like for you now, I hope you're happy.

Truly.

And if you ever happen to remember me, I hope it makes you smile.

Because despite everything, I am glad I knew you.

I'm glad we laughed.

I'm glad we shared the time we did.

And even if I sometimes wish things had been different, I wouldn't want to forget the good just to make letting go easier.

Maybe that's what acceptance looks like.

Not forgetting. Not pretending it didn't hurt.

Just being able to look back at something beautiful without needing it to happen again.

And being grateful that, for a little while, it happened at all.

Thank you for being here, even if only for a little while.
