A Blue Giant · November 10, 2026

For All the People I Couldn't Keep

Some constellations are only meant to hold for a little while.

I've been thinking about how strange it is to miss someone who is still somewhere in this world.

Someone who is still living their life, laughing, making memories, waking up every morning. Someone who hasn't really gone anywhere, except perhaps away from the place they once held in yours.

And I don't really know what to call that.

Maybe it's goodbye. Maybe it's acceptance. Or maybe it's simply learning that not everyone we love and care about is meant to walk beside us through every season of life.

I've been thinking about the people who have made my life a little brighter just by being in it.

The conversations, the laughter, the stupid little moments that probably meant nothing to anyone watching, but somehow meant the world to me.

The ordinary days that became beautiful memories without us even realizing it.

And I am grateful.

God, I am so grateful.

But I would be lying if I said that gratitude makes the sadness disappear.

Because sometimes I wish people could see themselves through my eyes. Just for a moment. To understand how precious they were to me. How much their presence meant. How genuinely I wanted to be part of their lives.

Not because I needed them to make me happy.

Not because I expected them to give me everything I gave.

But because I saw something beautiful in them, something worth appreciating, and I wanted to be there for it.

And maybe that's where things sometimes get complicated.

Because I care deeply. I always have.

When someone becomes important to me, I want them to know it. I want to celebrate their victories, listen to their stories, share the stupid things that happen throughout my day, and simply exist somewhere in their world.

Not every day. Not every moment.

Just somewhere.

A small, meaningful place in each other's lives.

I never wanted anyone to feel obligated to have me in their life. I just wanted to feel that they wanted me there.

And I think that's what hurts the most sometimes.

When a friendship that once felt so natural starts feeling like something you have to earn. When saying hello becomes something you overthink. When you find yourself wondering whether reaching out to someone you love will make them smile or make them feel bothered.

When something that once felt effortless slowly becomes something you approach with hesitation.

I never saw you as a burden, so it hurt to start wondering whether I had become one to you.

And maybe that feeling wasn't the truth. Maybe it was my own fears speaking louder than the reality in front of me.

I don't know.

But I know how it felt.

I know what it feels like to want to share something with someone and stop yourself because you're no longer sure whether your presence is welcome.

To have a thought, a story, a little piece of your day that reminds you of someone, and then realize you don't quite know where to put it anymore.

And that is a strange kind of sadness.

Because friendship, at least to me, was never about how often we spoke or how much time we spent together.

It was about knowing that somewhere in this enormous world, we had a little place in each other's lives.

A place where we belonged, even when life got busy.

A place we didn't constantly have to prove we deserved.

I think the hardest part has been learning that caring deeply about someone doesn't necessarily mean we'll find the same way of being present in each other's lives.

And that hurts.

Especially when you remember the seasons when everything felt different. When being part of each other's lives seemed so simple. When there was no question about whether you belonged.

I suppose that's what makes the distance so difficult to understand sometimes.

Not knowing how something that once felt so close could become so far away.

But I'm also learning something about myself.

That the way I love and care for people is part of who I am. That I don't need to become less affectionate, less thoughtful, or less willing to show up simply because someone else experiences friendship differently.

I don't want to lose the parts of myself that make me care so deeply, even when those parts have sometimes left me hurting.

And at the same time, I'm learning that I cannot expect everyone to receive my love the way I give it.

That people have their own lives, their own limits, their own ways of caring.

That someone can genuinely care about me and still be unable to offer the friendship I hoped we could have.

And maybe neither of us has to be wrong for that to be sad.

Maybe some friendships are beautiful precisely because of what they were, even if they couldn't become everything we wished they would be.

I'm learning that I can love people without holding on to them.

That I can appreciate everything we shared without needing more of it.

That the absence of someone doesn't erase the beauty of their presence.

And that two people can care about each other and still find themselves walking in different directions.

Perhaps that's one of the quieter lessons of growing older.

Learning that not every connection is meant to last forever, but that doesn't make it any less real.

So, to the people who have been part of my life, who made ordinary days extraordinary without even realizing it:

Thank you.

Thank you for the time, the laughter, the memories, and the little pieces of yourselves that you shared with me.

Thank you for the conversations that made the hours disappear.

For the moments when life felt lighter simply because you were there.

For the seasons when our lives found each other.

Even if those seasons were shorter than I wanted.

Even if I still wish things had been different.

Even if a part of me wishes I had felt a little more seen.

I hope life is gentle with you.

I hope you find the friendships, the love, and the happiness that make you feel understood, appreciated, and wanted.

I hope the people around you see the little things that make you who you are. The things you may not even notice about yourself, but that make you so wonderfully human.

And if our paths don't cross again, I hope you know that our time together mattered.

At least, it mattered to me.

And maybe that's enough.

I'm learning that I don't need to turn a beautiful memory into a painful one just because I wish there had been more of it.

That I can smile at what we shared, even if there's still a little sadness in remembering.

And perhaps the hardest lesson of all is learning to let people go without believing that their leaving means I wasn't worth staying for.

Because I was.

And so were they.

Maybe this isn't goodbye.

Maybe it's simply making peace with the fact that some people will always have a place in our stories, even if they no longer have a place in our everyday lives.

Maybe some people are meant to leave us with memories rather than promises.

And maybe that's okay.

Maybe the beautiful thing about having known someone isn't how long they stayed, but that, for a little while, out of all the people in this enormous world, our lives found each other.

And we got to share a piece of this strange, wonderful experience of being alive.

So wherever you are, whatever life looks like for you now, I hope you're happy.

Truly.

I hope life gives you reasons to smile, people who make you feel at home, and moments that remind you how beautiful it can be.

And if you ever happen to remember me, I hope those memories bring you warmth.

Because despite everything, that's what I want to carry with me, too.

Not the distance.

Not the silence.

Not the things we couldn't quite figure out.

But the good.

The laughter.

The little moments.

The simple happiness of having known you.

And perhaps, somewhere along the way, I'll learn that letting go doesn't always have to mean losing something.

Sometimes, it simply means being grateful that it happened at all.

Thank you for being here, even if only for a little while.
