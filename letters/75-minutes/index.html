75 Minutes

Dear you,

Seventy-five minutes.

That was the goal. A small line drawn against time. Something simple enough to say casually: Just do sub-75.

But I believed you could do it.

Today, from across an ocean, I watched you cross that line in 1:14:56. Seventy-five minutes, with four seconds to spare. Four seconds between almost and done. Four seconds that somehow felt like the perfect ending to our little tale.

September 6 has always been a big day for me. It is the day I got my first license—the first real symbol that I could move through the world on my own. It is the birthday of my first and greatest love. And now, it is also the day this bittersweet chapter ends.

I wanted to tell you good luck before your race. But the truth is that “good luck” was carrying much more than those two words should ever have to carry.

What I really wanted to tell you was that I was proud of you. That I was still thinking about you. That I remembered what you were working toward, because remembering has always been one of the biggest ways I know how to love someone.

I wanted to tell you that you still have an incredibly special place in my mind and in my life. That even from Italy, even with all this distance between us, a part of me was still quietly standing near that finish line, hoping you would do exactly what you did.

I helped in the small ways I still could. I gave you the ticket I had been saving. I made sure Aaron knew your start time so someone could capture the moment. I told you to go sub-75. Then I stepped away and let the rest belong to you.

You did it.

And I am so proud of you.

For a while, I believed caring meant continuing to show up. Another conversation. Another coffee flavor I thought you might like. Another small detail remembered. Another reason to reach toward you and say, I saw this and thought of you.

Those things came naturally to me because you mattered. You became part of the quiet movement of my mind—the place where a song, a run, a joke, or something completely ordinary could suddenly bring you back.

But when I needed you close, you pulled away.

I understand why. You did not want to be placed in the middle of a life and a relationship I was still trying to understand. You needed a boundary, and you had every right to create one.

Still, when you told me to come back when I was ready, it hurt.

It made me feel like I needed to become a particular version of myself before I could belong in your life again. Like the confused, hurting, unfinished version of me had to disappear first. I could have walked through almost anything if you had wanted me beside you, but when I was struggling to walk at all, I felt you release my hand.

I don’t say that with anger. I don’t think you intended to hurt me. I understand that protecting yourself sometimes means stepping away from someone you care about.

But understanding the boundary did not make the distance hurt any less.

Eventually, I realized I needed a boundary too.

Not because I stopped loving you. Not because I wanted to punish you or make you notice my absence. I needed distance because remaining emotionally close to you while feeling so far away was hurting me. I was standing with my heart open, waiting for a kind of closeness that was no longer available to me.

And waiting was not bringing me peace.

So I stopped reaching. I tried to let the silence be silence instead of treating it like a space I needed to fill. I started learning that I could care about you without constantly placing that care in your hands.

That is what today became.

I watched you reach the goal I had quietly hoped for. I let myself feel proud. I acknowledged it from the distance. And I did not ask you to turn around and find me there.

Maybe that is what these 75 minutes were meant to teach me.

You were running toward a finish line while I was finally accepting one.

I don’t want to erase you from my story. What I felt was real, even if it could not become what part of me once hoped it might become. Our conversations were real. The tenderness was real. The ways we cared were real. The hurt was real too.

You mattered to me. You still do.

But love does not always mean remaining close. Sometimes it means wishing someone well without asking to keep a place beside them. Sometimes it means allowing a story to end without rewriting it as something meaningless.

So this is where I leave ours.

At 1:14:56.

Four seconds beneath the goal.

On September 6, a day that has always carried freedom and love for me, and that will now also carry the memory of letting go.

I hope life is gentle with you. I hope you continue discovering how strong you are. I hope you keep surprising yourself. I hope the people beside you remember the small things and make you feel deeply known.

And wherever life takes us, I hope something ordinary brings me to your mind every once in a while. Not with guilt or sadness. Just with the quiet warmth of having once known someone who cared about you very much.

A part of me will always love you.

But I will love you from here now, without waiting for you to come closer.

You did it.

I’m so proud of you.

Goodbye, my friend.
