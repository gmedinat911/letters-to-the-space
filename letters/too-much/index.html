A Golden Pulsar · November 21, 2026

Too Much

On becoming the person I once needed, and fearing that even he might be too much.

Dear space,

There's something I've been trying to understand about myself.

At first, I thought it was anger.

Anger over a friendship I felt like I had to keep asking for. Over someone saying they wanted me in their life while making me feel like there wasn't really a place for me in it.

I wasn't asking to be someone's entire world.

I just wanted to be their friend.

Someone they could laugh with, talk to, be vulnerable with. Someone who mattered without having to constantly look for proof.

And maybe we just understood friendship differently.

Maybe what I wanted wasn't what they could give.

I can understand that.

But understanding something doesn't always make it hurt less.

And somewhere in all that anger, I started questioning something much bigger.

Myself.

The way I care.
The way I show up.
How I remember the little things.
How I try to make sure the people in my life never have to wonder if they matter to me.

I started wondering if all of it was too much.

If I was too available.
Too vulnerable.
Too willing to give.
Too much of everything.

And I think I know why that thought hurt so badly.

Because there was a time when I was so alone that the smallest bit of kindness could make me feel seen.

I remember what it felt like to have nobody to call.

To wish someone would remember me.

To want to matter to someone without having to ask.

And maybe I've spent so much of my life wishing someone would make me feel important that, when I finally had people to care about, I tried to make sure none of them would ever feel the way I did.

Maybe I became the person I once needed.

Someone who shows up.
Someone who listens.
Someone who cares, sometimes more than he knows how to explain.

And somewhere along the way, perhaps I started believing that if I cared enough, showed up enough, and made someone feel important enough, they would naturally want to stay.

Not because they owed me anything.

But because I thought that's what people did when they cared about each other.

And there's something I've been learning, space.

Something I wish I didn't have to.

You can give someone all the kindness you once wished someone had given you, and it still might not be what they need.

You can care deeply about someone and still not have the friendship you hoped for.

You can do your best and still lose people.

But I think what scares me most is what happens inside me when they leave.

Because I don't just question what happened between us.

I start questioning everything about myself.

I wonder if I should have reached out less.
Cared less.
Said less.
Needed less.

If maybe being a little less me would have made someone want to stay.

And that's a terrible thing to believe about myself.

Especially when I've spent so many years becoming someone I'm trying to be proud of.

I know I have things to learn.

That caring deeply doesn't mean everyone wants the same closeness.

That respecting someone's space doesn't mean abandoning my own needs.

That I can learn when to step back without becoming cold.

But knowing those things doesn't stop the sadness.

Because I think losing this friendship reminded me of a loneliness I thought I'd left behind.

And do you know what's strange?

I'm not that lonely person anymore.

There are people who love me.

People who choose me.

People who make room for me without making me wonder whether I belong.

But somehow, one person walking away made me forget all of that for a moment.

And suddenly I was that younger version of myself again.

Wondering what was wrong with me.

Wondering why being myself never seemed like enough.

I wish I could go back and sit beside him.

Tell him that one day he'll become someone who knows how to make others feel seen.

That he'll have people who genuinely want him around.

That he won't have to spend his whole life trying to earn a place in someone's world.

And that sometimes, even when he does everything with a good heart, someone still won't stay.

I wish I could tell him that doesn't mean he failed.

I really wish I could.

Because I think a part of me still believes that if I could just be enough for everyone, nobody would ever make me feel alone again.

And maybe that's what I need to unlearn.

Not how to care.

Not how to love people.

But how to stop making someone else's decision to stay or leave a measure of who I am.

I don't want to become smaller just because someone couldn't meet me where I was.

I don't want to apologize for every good thing about myself just because I haven't always known where to give it.

But some days, it's hard to remember any of that.

And I find myself thinking about the person I spent so many years becoming.

The little boy who once wanted nothing more than to feel like he mattered.

And the person who grew up trying to make sure nobody else ever had to feel that way.

I wish I could tell you that I've figured it out.

That I know how to care without being afraid of losing people.

That I know how to let someone leave without wondering what I should have done differently.

But I haven't.

And maybe that's why I'm telling you all of this.

Because after all these years, after all the people I've met, all the friendships I've made, and all the love I've been lucky enough to receive,

I still sometimes feel like that lonely kid,

hoping that if he's good enough to someone,

they'll want to stay.

And I don't know what to do with that feeling yet.

So, for now,

I'm leaving it here.

With you.
