When February Went Quiet

I thought stopping the wedding would tell me something about whether I loved you. Somewhere in my head, I had made those two things inseparable. If I loved you, I should want the wedding. If I questioned the wedding, then I must be questioning you. And if part of me felt relieved by stopping it, I didn’t really know what that was supposed to mean.

But something strange happened. We stopped the wedding, and I think I started seeing you more clearly.

I don’t know if it was the pressure disappearing, or the date disappearing, or suddenly not having this enormous thing in front of us that we constantly had to measure our relationship against. Every good moment didn’t have to mean, see, we’re going to be okay. Every bad moment didn’t have to mean, maybe we shouldn’t be getting married. For the first time in a long time, February 18 wasn’t sitting in the room with us.

And you were still there.

More importantly, I still wanted you there.

That’s the part I wasn’t expecting. I thought maybe removing the wedding would expose some truth I had been avoiding. Maybe I would suddenly feel free. Maybe the relief would answer the question for me and I would realize that what I wanted was a life without you.

But that isn’t what happened.

I felt relieved, yes. Very relieved. But not relieved to be away from you. Relieved that I could finally look at you without having to decide the rest of my life at the same time.

And somehow, that has made me appreciate you more. The little things. The way you care. The history between us. The life we’ve already built that existed long before there was a venue or a date on a calendar. Even the things about you that drive me crazy somehow feel different when I’m not quietly asking myself what every single thing means for forever.

I think I had been asking one question as if it were the only question: Do I want to marry you?

Maybe I needed to pull it apart.

Do I want to get married? I don’t know.

Do I know what our future looks like? I don’t know.

Do I know whether we can work through the things that brought us here? I don’t know that either.

But do I love you?

That one suddenly feels much easier.

Yes.

I love you.

And I don’t mean that as an answer to all the other questions. Maybe that’s the realization. Love doesn’t answer them. It doesn’t automatically mean we should get married. It doesn’t make our problems disappear. It doesn’t guarantee that everything works out exactly how we once imagined it.

It just means I love you.

And somehow, taking the wedding away has allowed that to exist without needing it to prove anything.

Maybe February 18 had become so loud that I couldn’t hear that anymore.

Now it’s quieter.

And there you are.

And here I am.

Still loving you.

Maybe, for right now, that is enough.
