The Raffle

Dear Grace,

I never win anything.

Apparently, that was one of the first things I ever told you. There was a raffle, my name got pulled out of something, and then one morning a stranger named Grace texted me to tell me I had won a free evaluation.

That was it. Nothing cinematic. No universe splitting open. No feeling that something important had just happened. Just a text from someone I didn't know yet.

I think there is something beautiful about that now.

I've been thinking lately about how people find each other. Some people enter your life loudly. There is a moment you can point to and say, there, that's where everything started. You know something important is happening while it's happening.

And then there are people who enter through some side door you didn't even know was open. They arrive so ordinarily that you don't think to mark the day. There is no reason to. They're just another person you met, another name in your phone, another life briefly crossing yours.

Until they aren't.

They start appearing in more places. Conversations get longer. Jokes become inside jokes. You start knowing things about each other that have nothing to do with the reason you originally met. And one day, without any ceremony at all, you realize this person who used to exist somewhere near the edges of your life has somehow become part of it.

I don't know exactly when that happened with you.

I don't know when you stopped being the person I went to because something hurt and became someone I wanted to talk to even when nothing did. I don't know when I stopped thinking of you as my PT and started thinking of you as my friend. Or when my friend quietly became something closer to one of my people.

Maybe there wasn't a moment.

Maybe friendship isn't always built from big moments. Maybe sometimes it's just a thousand ordinary ones accumulating so quietly that neither person notices they're building something until one day it's already there.

A workout. A race. A ridiculous conversation. A serious conversation that wasn't supposed to become serious. Talking about our goals. Talking about life. Talking about work. Talking complete shit. Sending each other something completely random because, for whatever reason, it made us think of the other person.

Somewhere inside all of that, you became important to me.

And I think that's one of the strangest and most beautiful things about being alive.

There are billions of people moving through the world at the same time. Entire lives happening beside ours that we'll never know anything about. People laughing somewhere. People struggling. People falling in love. People starting over. People chasing something. People becoming versions of themselves they haven't met yet.

Most of those lives will never touch ours.

And then, every once in a while, somehow, two of them intersect.

Usually for reasons that seem completely insignificant at the time.

A place. A job. A race.

A raffle.

And suddenly someone who was once a complete stranger becomes a person whose happiness matters to you.

That's what happened with you.

Somewhere along the way, your wins started making me happy. Your goals became things I wanted to see you accomplish. Your dreams became things I genuinely wanted to watch become real. And your friendship became something I started carrying with me without ever consciously deciding to.

I think that's how I know when someone matters to me.

I remember them.

Not only the important things. Actually, mostly the little things. The things people probably don't expect anyone to keep. A sentence. A joke. Something they were worried about. Something they mentioned wanting years from now. The way they sounded when they were excited about something. The version of them that existed in a completely ordinary moment.

I keep those things.

I always have.

It's one of the ways I love people.

And somewhere along the way, I started keeping pieces of you too. I remember the things you want for yourself. I remember the things that worry you. I remember what gets you excited. I remember the ridiculous conversations and the serious ones and all the ones that somehow became serious even though neither of us intended them to.

I remember you believing in me.

And I hope you know that I believe in you too.

Not just athlete Grace. Not just PT Grace. Not just the version of you who is accomplishing something or chasing the next goal.

Just Grace.

You.

Because you've gotten to see a lot of versions of me too. The ambitious one. The injured one. The ridiculously overconfident one. The one who signs up for things first and figures out whether they were good ideas afterward. The one who needs you to somehow fix whatever part of his body he has most recently decided to destroy.

But you've also seen quieter versions of me. Versions that weren't doing particularly well. Versions that doubted themselves. Versions that were sad. Versions that didn't necessarily need someone to fix anything.

Just someone there.

And I don't know if you realize how much I appreciate the people who make it feel safe for those versions of me to exist.

You have.

There have been moments when something you said probably felt completely ordinary to you and stayed with me anyway.

That's the funny thing about kindness. The person giving it rarely knows how far it travels. Sometimes you say something and forget it five minutes later.

Meanwhile, someone else carries it home.

I've carried some of yours.

And I hope somewhere along the way I've given you a few things worth carrying too.

I hope I've been someone you can be ridiculous with. Someone you can tell your dreams to before they're completely formed. Someone who believes you when you say you want something bigger. Someone who will remind you of who you are on the days when you temporarily forget.

Someone who will happily become Coach Gabe despite having absolutely no qualifications whatsoever.

And someone who will still be cheering when you eventually build the career you've talked about.

For the record, your position at the imaginary performance center is still reserved. You called dibs. Some promises are sacred.

But underneath all the jokes and training and whatever terrible physical decision we're enabling each other to make next, there is something I don't think I tell you enough.

I'm really, really grateful that you're here.

There are people we love because they've been with us forever. They know old versions of us. They remember who we were before we became whoever we are now.

And then there are people who remind you that life isn't finished introducing you to people you're going to love.

I think that's beautiful.

Because it means some of our people are still out there.

Some of the people who will make us laugh until we can't breathe are currently strangers. Some of the people who will someday sit beside us during difficult seasons don't know our names yet. Some of the people whose victories will someday make us genuinely happy are out there living entire lives completely separate from ours.

Until, somehow, they're not.

Once, you were one of those people.

There was a version of my life where I didn't know Grace Parker.

That's strange to think about now.

There was a version of me who didn't know your laugh, your ambition, your kindness, your ridiculousness, your determination. A version of me who didn't know that one day I'd care whether you reached your goals or whether you were doing okay. A version who couldn't possibly have known that someday I'd be sitting here trying to find words big enough to explain how grateful I am for your friendship.

That version of my life existed.

And then, completely randomly, it didn't anymore.

A name came out of a raffle.

A stranger sent me a text.

And life quietly became different by one person.

You.

I don't know how long people get to walk beside each other in this life. I'm learning not to assume forever. Life changes too quickly for that. People move. People grow. Circumstances change. Sometimes people who once knew everything about each other eventually know very little.

I used to think that possibility made loving people frightening.

Maybe it still does a little.

But I'm starting to think the uncertainty is exactly why we should tell people what they mean to us while they're here.

Not someday. Not when there's a reason. Not when something is ending.

While they're here.

So while you're here, Grace, I want you to know.

I see you. I appreciate you. I believe in you. I'm proud of you.

And I love you, my friend.

Not because of what you do for me. Not because you can put my body back together after I inevitably do something stupid. Not because we train together. Not because of HYROX or races or any of the things that happened to put us in the same rooms.

Just because somewhere between that first text and now, you became someone I care about very deeply.

And I'm really glad life let me know you.

Which brings me back to the beginning.

I never win anything.

That's what I said.

And maybe most of the time that's true. But every once in a while, I think life gives you something without telling you what it's worth yet.

It lets you think you've won one thing.

Then you live a little longer.

You laugh together. You struggle together. You learn each other. You show up. You collect a thousand completely ordinary moments that don't seem like anything special while they're happening.

And then one day you look backward and realize they weren't ordinary at all.

They were a friendship becoming a friendship.

So I think I owe that version of myself a correction.

I did win something that day.

I just didn't know what I had won yet.

Love you, Grace.

Gabe
