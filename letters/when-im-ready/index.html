When I’m Ready

I think some sentences hurt because they are kind.

There is nothing cruel about I’ll be here when you’re ready.

Maybe that’s why I haven’t known what to do with it.

Because I understand.

I understand that nobody should have to carry the weight of another person.

I understand that caring about someone doesn’t make you responsible for saving them.

I understand that sometimes space is the healthiest thing two people can give each other.

I understand all of it.

And somehow, it still hurts.

Because I don’t know what ready means.

Does it mean when I’m okay again?

When I’ve untangled everything?

When I can talk about my life without accidentally handing you pieces of it to carry?

When I don’t need reassurance that you’re still there?

Maybe.

But there’s a small part of me that heard something completely different.

Come back when you’re easier to hold.

And I know that’s probably unfair.

Those weren’t your words.

They were mine.

Or maybe they belonged to a much younger version of me who learned somewhere along the way that people are easier to keep when you don’t need too much from them.

So now I’m doing the thing you asked me to do.

I’m stepping back.

Not because I’m angry.

Not because I stopped caring.

God, I wish caring were that easy to turn off.

I’m stepping back because I care enough to understand that someone’s boundary isn’t an invitation to convince them otherwise.

But there’s something neither of us can know yet.

You said you’d be there when I’m ready.

And maybe you will.

I just don’t know who I’ll be when that happens.

Maybe I’ll come back and we’ll laugh about stupid things again.

Maybe everything will feel familiar.

Maybe I’ll finally understand what you meant.

Or maybe, somewhere between here and ready, I’ll learn how to stop leaving so much of myself in other people’s hands.

And when I finally look around, the space you once occupied will have changed.

Not because I wanted to erase you.

Not because what we had wasn’t real.

But because while I was learning how to stand on my own, I had to learn how to live without reaching for you.

That’s the strange thing about asking someone to come back when they’re ready.

Sometimes becoming ready changes where home is.

So I won’t ask you to wait.

And I won’t promise I’ll come back exactly the same.

I’ll just go do the work.

And if someday we find each other again,

I hope we recognize each other.
